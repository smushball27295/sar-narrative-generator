## Description

Structuring is the practice of breaking cash transactions into smaller amounts so that no single transaction triggers the currency transaction report that United States institutions file for cash transactions above $10,000. Structuring is an offence in its own right, whatever the source of the funds. It usually appears as repeated cash deposits or withdrawals just below the threshold by one customer, on the same day or on consecutive days, sometimes at several branches.

## Red-flag indicators

1. **STR-01** Multiple cash deposits in amounts just below the $10,000 currency transaction reporting threshold
2. **STR-02** Cash deposits by the same customer at several branches or ATMs on the same day or on consecutive days that together exceed the reporting threshold
3. **STR-03** Cash activity that is inconsistent with the customer's stated occupation, income or expected account activity
4. **STR-04** Customer asks about reporting or identification requirements, or changes or cancels a transaction after learning that a report would be filed
5. **STR-05** Frequent cash purchases of monetary instruments such as cashier's checks or money orders in amounts below the $3,000 record-keeping threshold
6. **STR-06** Repeated cash withdrawals just under the reporting threshold that together move a large sum out of the account in currency

## What the narrative should establish

- Who conducted the cash transactions and whether that person is the account holder
- The number, amounts and dates of the cash transactions and their aggregate value
- Which branches or ATMs were used and whether several were used on the same day
- How the cash activity compares with the customer's occupation and expected activity
