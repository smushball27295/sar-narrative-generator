## Description

Trade-based money laundering disguises criminal proceeds as payment for goods. Common techniques are over-invoicing and under-invoicing, invoicing the same shipment more than once, phantom shipments and misdescription of goods. Payments often arrive from third parties that are not party to the trade, and the documents do not agree with each other or with the customer's business.

## Red-flag indicators

1. **TBM-01** Payments for goods come from third parties that have no connection to the transaction or are not named in the trade documents
2. **TBM-02** The price or quantity of goods on invoices is significantly inconsistent with market value, indicating over-invoicing or under-invoicing
3. **TBM-03** The goods described are inconsistent with the customer's known line of business
4. **TBM-04** The same shipment is invoiced more than once, or shipping documents such as bills of lading are missing or inconsistent
5. **TBM-05** Trade payments are routed through jurisdictions with no apparent link to the origin or destination of the shipment
6. **TBM-06** Payments arrive in round amounts or instalments that do not correspond to invoice values

## What the narrative should establish

- The goods, invoice values and named parties on the trade documents
- Who actually paid and whether the payers appear on the documents
- How payments received compare with the invoiced amounts
- Whether the goods fit the customer's stated line of business and which jurisdictions were involved
