title: Shell company layering
source: FinCEN guidance on money laundering risks related to shell companies; FATF reports on concealment of beneficial ownership

## Description

A shell company is a legal entity with no significant operations or assets. Shell companies have lawful uses, but they also allow funds to be moved through layers of entities that hide who owns and controls the money. Typical activity is a recently formed company whose account is funded and emptied by wire transfers with other legal entities, with payment details that are vague and no sign of ordinary business expenses.

## Red-flag indicators

1. **SHL-01** Recently formed company with no visible operations: no payroll, premises, website or ordinary business expenses
2. **SHL-02** Account funded and depleted by wire transfers to and from other legal entities, with few or no payments to recognisable suppliers or customers
3. **SHL-03** Several companies share the same address, registered agent, directors or signers
4. **SHL-04** Payment details are vague, for example "consulting services" or "loan", and the amounts are round
5. **SHL-05** Transfers move among related entities in a circular pattern, with funds returning to the originator
6. **SHL-06** Beneficial ownership is unclear or held through nominees, and wires involve jurisdictions known for corporate secrecy

## What the narrative should establish

- When and where the company was formed and what is known about its owners and operations
- The entities on each side of the transfers and any shared addresses, agents or signers
- The stated purpose of the payments and whether any documents support it
- Whether funds returned to an earlier sender or moved in a circle
