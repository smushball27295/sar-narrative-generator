## Description

An off-ramp is the point where virtual currency is converted into ordinary money. Proceeds of fraud, ransomware or darknet sales are sold through exchanges or peer-to-peer traders, the payouts land in a bank account, and the funds are then withdrawn in cash or wired on. The bank sees only the last step, so the pattern of exchange payouts and what follows them carries most of the information.

## Red-flag indicators

1. **VCO-01** Frequent or large transfers from virtual-currency exchanges into a bank account, followed by prompt cash withdrawals or outgoing wires
2. **VCO-02** Customer receives funds from several exchanges or peer-to-peer trading platforms without a stated trading business or matching income
3. **VCO-03** Customer appears to operate as an unregistered peer-to-peer exchanger: many small incoming payments from individuals matched by transfers to exchanges
4. **VCO-04** Exchange payouts arrive soon after the account is opened or after a long period of dormancy
5. **VCO-05** Proceeds are immediately wired abroad or moved to accounts of unrelated third parties
6. **VCO-06** Customer is unable or unwilling to explain the source of the virtual currency, or blockchain analysis links it to mixers, darknet markets or ransomware

## What the narrative should establish

- Which exchanges paid the account, how often and in what amounts
- What happened to the funds after each payout and how quickly
- The customer's stated occupation and any explanation for holding virtual currency
- Whether the customer also collects payments from individuals and sends funds to exchanges
