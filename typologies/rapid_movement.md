title: Rapid movement of funds through a pass-through account

## Description

In a pass-through pattern an account acts as a conduit. Funds are wired in and wired out again on the same day or the next day in nearly equal amounts, the balance returns to a low level after each cycle, and the transfers have no apparent business purpose. The pattern is a layering technique: each hop adds distance between the funds and their origin.

## Red-flag indicators

1. **RPM-01** Incoming wire transfers followed within a day or two by outgoing wire transfers of nearly the same amount
2. **RPM-02** Account balance returns close to zero after each cycle and the account shows little other activity
3. **RPM-03** Wires originate from and are sent to parties with no apparent connection to the customer's line of business
4. **RPM-04** Transfers routed through several accounts or foreign jurisdictions with no economic rationale
5. **RPM-05** Wire amounts are large, round sums with vague or missing payment details
6. **RPM-06** Volume of wire activity far exceeds what was anticipated when the account was opened

## What the narrative should establish

- The originators and beneficiaries of the wires and their relationship to the customer, if any
- The timing of each inbound and outbound pair and how closely the amounts match
- The jurisdictions the funds came from and went to
- Whether the customer gave any business explanation or supporting documents
